The Song of Moses and the Lamb

(Deuteronomy 32:1–47)

1Then I saw another great and marvelous sign in heaven: seven angels with the seven final plagues, with which the wrath of God is completed.

2And I saw something like a sea of glass mixed with fire, beside which stood those who had conquered the beast and its image and the number of its name. They were holding harps from God, 3and they sang the song of God’s servant Moses and of the Lamb:

“Great and wonderful are Your works,

O Lord God Almighty!

Just and true are Your ways,

O King of the nations!1

4Who will not fear You, O Lord,

and glorify Your name?

For You alone are holy.

All nations will come and worship before You,

for Your righteous acts have been revealed.”

Preparation for Judgment

5After this I looked, and the temple—the tabernacle of the Testimony—was opened in heaven. 6And out of the temple2 came the seven angels with the seven plagues, dressed in clean and bright linen and girded with golden sashes around their chests.

7Then one of the four living creatures gave the seven angels seven golden bowls full of the wrath of God, who lives forever and ever. 8And the temple was filled with smoke from the glory of God and from His power; and no one could enter the temple until the seven plagues of the seven3 angels were completed.

Notes

1 15:3 SBL and WH King of the ages; TR King of the saints

2 15:6 F35 out of heaven

3 15:8 F35 does not include seven.

Catechism questions on this chapter

Read the chapter, then ask the questions. Choose your catechism.

A Catechism for Boys and Girls2 questions

  1. Q. 60

    Why is Christ a king?

    15:3–4

    Because He rules over us and defends us.

  2. Q. 77

    What does the Third Commandment teach us?

    15:3–4

    To reverence God’s name, Word, and works.

The Baptist Catechism2 questions

  1. Q. 7

    What is God?

    15:4

    God is a spirit, infinite, eternal, and unchangeable, in his being, wisdom, power, holiness, justice, goodness and truth.

  2. Q. 59

    What is required in the third commandment?

    15:3–4

    The third commandment requireth, the holy and reverent use of God’s names, titles, attributes, ordinances, word, and works.
