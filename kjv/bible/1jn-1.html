The Word of Life

(Luke 24:36–49; John 20:19–23)

1That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, of the Word of life; 2(For the life was manifested, and we have seen it, and bear witness, and shew unto you that eternal life, which was with the Father, and was manifested unto us;) 3That which we have seen and heard declare we unto you, that ye also may have fellowship with us: and truly our fellowship is with the Father, and with his Son Jesus Christ. 4And these things write we unto you, that your joy may be full.

Walking in the Light

(John 8:12–29)

5This then is the message which we have heard of him, and declare unto you, that God is light, and in him is no darkness at all. 6If we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in darkness, we lie, and do not the truth: 7But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin. 8If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. 9If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness. 10If we say that we have not sinned, we make him a liar, and his word is not in us.

Catechism questions on this chapter

Read the chapter, then ask the questions. Choose your catechism.

A Catechism for Boys and Girls2 questions

  1. Q. 62

    Why do you need Christ as a priest?

    1:8–9

    Because I am guilty.

  2. Q. 95

    Can any man keep these Ten Commandments perfectly?

    1:8, 1:10

    No mere man, since the fall of Adam, ever did or can keep the Ten Commandments perfectly.

The Baptist Catechism1 question

  1. Q. 87

    Is any man able perfectly to keep the commandments of God?

    1:8, 1:10

    No meer man since the fall, is able in this life, perfectly to keep the commandments of God, but doth daily break them, in thought, work, or deed.

An Orthodox Catechism7 questions

  1. Q. 1

    What is thy only comfort in life and death?

    1:7

    That both in soul and body, whether I live or die, I am not mine own, but belong wholly unto my most faithful Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ: who by his most precious blood fully satisfying for all my sins, hath delivered me from all the power of the devil, and so preserveth me, that without the will of my heavenly Father in heaven not so much as an hair may fall from my head; yea all things must serve for my safety: wherefore by his Spirit also he, assureth me of everlasting life, and maketh me ready and prepared, that henceforth I may live to him.

  2. Q. 5

    Art thou able to keep all these things perfectly?

    1:8

    No truly: for by nature I am prone to the hatred of God, and of my neighbours.

  3. Q. 17

    Why must he also be very God?

    1:2

    That he might by the power of his Godhead sustain in his flesh the burthen of God’s wrath, and might recover and restore unto us that righteousness and life which we lost.

  4. Q. 32

    For what cause is Christ called the only begotten Son of God, when we also are the sons of God?

    1:3

    Because Christ alone is the eternal and natural Son of the eternal Father, and we are but sons adopted of the Father by grace for his sake.

  5. Q. 54

    What mean those words, the communion of saints?

    the whole chapter

    First, that all and every one who believeth, are in common partakers of Christ and all his graces, as being his members; and then that every one ought readily and cheerfully bestow the gifts and graces which they have received to the common commodity and safety of all.

  6. Q. 77

    Is then the outward baptism of water the washing away of sins?

    1:7

    It is not; for the blood of Christ alone cleanseth us from all sin.

  7. Q. 137

    Why will God then have his law to be so exactly and severely preached seeing there is no man in this life able to keep it?

    1:9

    First, that all our life time we more & more acknowledge the great proneness of our nature to sin, and so much the more greedily desire remission of sins, and righteousness in Christ. Secondly, that we be doing of this always, and always thinking of it, and so implore and crave of the Father the grace of his Holy Spirit, whereby we may daily more and more be renewed to the image and likeness of God, until at length after we are departed out of this life we may joyfully attain unto that perfection which is promised unto us.
